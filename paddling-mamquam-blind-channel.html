Paddling the Mamquam Blind Channel

A calm tidal channel right behind downtown Squamish, a short paddle from coffee. What to expect, how to time it, and how to share it with boats.

Most Squamish paddles need a drive. The Mamquam Blind Channel doesn't. It's a calm, tidal channel tucked behind downtown, with mountain views and a coffee shop a few minutes away. It's the easy, close-to-home cousin of the estuary.

What it's like

Narrow and sheltered, with forest and town on either side and mountains in the background. It's flatwater on a calm day, which makes it a good spot for a first paddleboard session, a quick evening paddle, or an hour before work.

It's tidal

Like the estuary, the channel rises and falls with the tide. At higher tides there's more water, easier launching and more channel to explore. At low tide, edges turn to mud and some areas get shallow. Check the tide table (see reading the tide) and aim for a window around high tide.

Share it with boats

The southern end of the channel is near the harbour and marinas, so you'll share the water with powerboats.

  • Stay to the side of the channel and out of the way of boats.
  • Be visible. Bright clothing helps.
  • Carry a whistle (required in Canada on a canoe or paddleboard, along with a PFD).
  • Turn your bow into larger wakes rather than taking them side-on.

Wind

The channel is more sheltered than open Howe Sound, but Squamish's summer afternoon winds can still push you around near the open end. Mornings and evenings are calmest. If you see kites out on the Spit, the wind is up.

Safety basics

  • PFD on, done up. The water is cold salt water year-round.
  • Dress for immersion in cooler months.
  • Tell someone your plan.
  • Stay off the water in fog or strong wind.

Combine it with the estuary

Comfortable paddlers on a big tide and a calm day can explore further toward the estuary channels. If you do, the house rules of a refuge apply: keep to open channels, give birds a wide berth, and stay out of the sedge. See birdwatching in the estuary.

Getting a boat there

Squamish Canoe Rental delivers canoes and paddleboards to the Mamquam Blind Channel, with paddles, PFDs and safety gear included, or you can pick up at their Squamish base.

Questions

Where is the Mamquam Blind Channel?

It's a calm tidal channel that runs along the back of downtown Squamish toward the head of Howe Sound, close to the harbour and the estuary.

Is the Mamquam Blind Channel good for beginners?

Yes, on a calm day at the right tide it's sheltered flatwater. Watch for boat traffic near the harbour, wear a PFD, and avoid windy afternoons.

Can I rent a canoe or paddleboard for the Blind Channel?

Yes. Squamish Canoe Rental delivers canoes and paddleboards to the Mamquam Blind Channel, or you can pick up gear at their Squamish base.
