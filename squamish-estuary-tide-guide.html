Reading the Tide for the Estuary

The estuary is the easiest paddle in Squamish, if the water's there. How to read a tide table, pick your window, and get home before the mud or the wind.

The Squamish Estuary (Skwelwil'em) is flat, close to town and beautiful. It's also tidal. Twice a day the sea floods into the grassy channels, and twice a day it drains back out. Get the timing right and it's the easiest paddle in the corridor. Get it wrong and you're dragging a canoe across mud.

Why the tide matters so much here

At low tide, many of the channels drain to mudflats. At high tide, the water fills the channels and spreads into the sedge meadows. Your paddle depends on being out there when the water is in.

How to read a tide table

Look up the official Canadian Hydrographic Service tide tables from Fisheries and Oceans Canada (tides.gc.ca), or a tide app that uses that data, and choose the station nearest Squamish. For your day, you'll see:

  • Times of high and low tide. Usually two of each a day.
  • Heights, in metres. Not all high tides are equal. A 4 m+ high gives a comfortable window; a low high tide is a short one.

Picking your window

  1. Find the high tide that falls in daylight, ideally in the morning.
  2. Launch within an hour or two of high tide. Water in the grass, plenty of room in the channels.
  3. Aim to head back while the tide is still in, ideally while it's still coming in, so the flood carries you home rather than the ebb stranding you.
  4. Leave a margin. If you're running late, turn back early. The water drains faster than you'd think.

The other half: wind

Squamish isn't the kiteboarding capital of Canada by accident. On many summer afternoons a strong wind builds up Howe Sound and turns flat water into chop. Mornings and evenings are usually calm. The ideal estuary paddle is a morning high tide. If you see kites and sails out on the Spit, the wind's up.

A simple plan

  • Check the tide table and marine forecast the night before.
  • Pick a morning high tide of a good height.
  • Launch an hour or so before high water.
  • Paddle out with the last of the flood, explore around the top of the tide, and be heading back before the afternoon wind.
  • Lifejackets on, not under the seat. It's cold salt water.

If you get caught out

If the water drops faster than you planned, head for the main channels, which hold water longest. Avoid walking on estuary mud if you can; it can be deep and soft. Don't cut through the sedge meadows, which are nesting and rearing habitat.

Questions

What tide do you need to paddle the Squamish Estuary?

Plan to launch within an hour or two of high tide so there's water in the channels and grass. A high tide of around 4 m or more gives a comfortable window; a low high tide gives you much less time.

Where can I find tide tables for Squamish?

Use the official Canadian Hydrographic Service tide tables from Fisheries and Oceans Canada (tides.gc.ca) and pick the station nearest Squamish, or any reliable tide app that uses that data.

What happens if you paddle the estuary at low tide?

The channels drain to mud and you can end up stranded or dragging your canoe across the flats. Walking on estuary mud can also be hazardous. Time your trip around high tide.
